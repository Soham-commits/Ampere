# AI options for Ampere

Ampere’s route comparison uses seeded charger locations. Effort ratings are relative engineering estimates. Demo examples assume a selected vehicle and clearly labeled sample inputs.

| Option | What it does | Data needed | Effort and trade-off | Demo value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Natural-language trip requests | Converts “get me to Mumbai with at least 20% battery left” into a destination and arrival reserve. Shows the interpreted inputs for confirmation, then passes them to the route planner. | Supported corridors and city aliases, vehicle profile, departure battery, and a schema for trip constraints. | Low to medium. Flexible input is easy to demonstrate. Ambiguous places and missing inputs need clarification, and the planner must enforce every accepted constraint. | High. Judges can enter the same trip with different arrival reserves and see the plan change or explain an infeasible request. |
| Personalized charging-stop optimization | Chooses charging stops and target charge levels based on the EV, starting charge, arrival reserve, and estimated charging time. | Usable battery capacity, consumption, connector and power limits, charging curves, and distances between compatible stations. | Medium. A deterministic optimizer can produce explainable plans with seeded data. Accurate time estimates depend on charging curves and verified charger data. | High. A low-battery Nexon and a higher-range EV can get different stop sequences on Pune → Delhi. |
| Charger uptime and queue prediction | Estimates the chance of finding a working, available connector at the driver’s expected arrival time. | Historical status by connector, successful sessions, occupancy or queue observations, timestamps, and coverage by operator. | High. Sparse or stale reports make predictions hard to calibrate. Show confidence and the age of observations where sufficient data exists. | Medium. Simulated history can demonstrate ranking, while predictive quality requires real observations and backtesting. |
| Condition-aware range estimation | Adjusts expected energy consumption for traffic, climbing, weather, and air conditioning. Shows an arrival battery range. | Route elevation and speed profile, weather, vehicle efficiency, and measured consumption under similar conditions. | Medium to high. A simple physics model can start with few inputs. A trained model needs representative labeled trips and validation across vehicles. | Medium to high. A scenario comparison can show how a climb and higher AC load change charging needs. |

## Recommendation

Start with **natural-language trip requests**. Ampere already has vehicle inputs, corridor comparison, and a battery-aware planner. A language model would translate the request into a small set of validated fields. The planner would still calculate energy use, select stops, and report coverage gaps.

A first demo could ask for Pune → Mumbai with at least 20% remaining. Show the interpreted parameters before planning, and explain any shortfall in terms of the modeled arrival battery or the first unreachable leg. Ampere’s existing planner uses a fixed 10% reserve, so supporting a requested minimum arrival percentage is part of this future work. Test the structured output on ambiguous cities, missing departure battery, unsupported corridors, and impossible reserves before connecting it to the UI.
